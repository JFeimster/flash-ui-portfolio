Consumer Packaged Goods

M&M’s

Two candy characters turn a simple product into a running comedy act.

The Red and Yellow characters keep the M&M’s brand recognizable through personality clashes, awkward situations, and playful exchanges. Rather than depending on product features alone, the campaigns make the characters themselves a reason to remember the candy.

Character-led storytellingSituational comedy

Consumer Packaged Goods

Old Spice

Old Spice turns grooming advertisements into spectacular nonsense.

Old Spice campaigns pivot from ordinary grooming promises to rapid-fire absurdity, impossible scene changes, and confidently ridiculous characters. The contrast between straight-faced delivery and surreal spectacle keeps the product in the joke without making it the only point.

Absurdist escalationRapid scene changes
▶ Watch campaign ↗
Consumer Packaged Goods

Liquid Death

Liquid Death sells canned water with exaggerated anti-boring theatrics.

TalktoZilly contrasts Liquid Death's more confrontational humor with softer Jester approaches. Its deliberately extreme naming and irreverent presentation demonstrate how the tone itself can become a differentiator in a commodity category.

Provocative humorGenre inversion

Beauty & Personal Care

Dollar Shave Club

Dollar Shave Club uses irreverence to challenge grooming conventions.

Instead of the usual polished grooming pitch, its best-known early advertising leaned into brash understatement and an intentionally unglamorous presentation. That disruptive tone positioned an everyday subscription as a funny alternative to category clichés.

Anti-category comedyDirect response humor

Insurance

GEICO

GEICO makes a serious insurance purchase easier to remember with comedy.

GEICO repeatedly places familiar situations at a strange angle: a gecko with opinions, a camel celebrating hump day, or unexpected celebrity appearances. The comedic premise becomes the entry point while the insurance offer remains recognizable.

Perspective shiftComic spokescharacters

Insurance

Progressive

Progressive uses a recurring character to humanize insurance.

Progressive's long-running comedic persona is regularly cited in Jester branding discussions because it gives an otherwise complex service a familiar face. The humor makes comparison shopping feel less forbidding without replacing the underlying coverage decision.

Recurring comic characterFriendly tension relief

B2B & SaaS

Mailchimp

Mailchimp gives workplace marketing tools an approachable personality.

Apricot lists Mailchimp among brands with Jester tendencies. A colorful identity and playful product communication illustrate how software can avoid feeling sterile; the source provides a broad archetype classification rather than proof that every campaign uses comedy.

Friendly product personalityWhimsical presentation

Hospitality, Travel & QSR

Wendy’s

Wendy’s converts fast-food competition into a source of jokes.

Wendy’s has used memorable ad lines and punchy social replies to give the brand a sharper personality than a standard restaurant promotion. The studied examples focus on the comedic contrast between conventional fast-food marketing and the brand’s witty attitude.

Competitive witSocial banter

Quick-Service Restaurants

Taco Bell

Taco Bell makes room for wit even in the small details.

From sauce-packet copy to group-meal naming and playful product presentation, Taco Bell's example shows how a mischievous tone can extend beyond a single advertisement. Humor works as a recognizable through-line rather than just an isolated punchline.

Playful microcopyEveryday irreverence

Kitchen Appliances

Blendtec

Blendtec made product demonstrations into absurd experiments.

The 'Will It Blend?' campaigns recast a straightforward blender demonstration as a suspenseful question about wildly unexpected objects. The joke also demonstrates product capability, which makes the absurdity more useful than a disconnected gag.

Product demonstration as spectacleUnexpected premises

Food & Ice Cream

Ben & Jerry’s

Ben & Jerry’s represents a softer, communal mode of Jester branding.

TalktoZilly uses Ben & Jerry’s to contrast a gentler, good-natured playful voice with more abrasive comic brands. This is an interpretation of a style of messaging, not a claim that every part of the company's identity is defined by the Jester.

Warm playfulnessApproachable wordplay

Beverages

Budweiser

Budweiser turned a casual greeting into a shared advertising joke.

Apricot points to the 'Wassup' campaign as an example of sociable, repeatable humor. Its most memorable feature was a line that audiences could perform themselves, making the advertisement part of everyday conversation.

Shared catchphraseSocial participation

Snickers has been cited for humorous need-state advertising.

Apricot includes Snickers among its Jester examples. The source establishes an archetype association, but does not document a complete history of its campaign strategy; this profile therefore focuses on the recognizable practice of making hunger an exaggerated social reversal.
