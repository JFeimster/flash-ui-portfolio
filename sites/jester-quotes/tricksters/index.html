The trickster files.

139 possibilities. Gods, rebels, comedians, shape-shifters and fictional troublemakers. Some are established tricksters; others are interpretations worth questioning.

001

Aang from Avatar: The Last Airbender/Legend of Korra

Aang often answers danger with jokes, movement and inventive detours, even while carrying the grave responsibilities of the Avatar. His playfulness sits beside, rather than replaces, his heroism.

✳
002

Ace Ventura

Ace's exaggerated mannerisms, mimicry and refusal to behave like an ordinary detective upend authority and social expectations. The comedy is often deliberately obnoxious.

✳
003

Ame-No-Uzume-No-Mikoto, Goddess

Ame-no-Uzume's exuberant dance helps lure the hidden sun goddess Amaterasu out of her cave. Her disruptive performance restores light rather than merely provoking mischief.

004

Amelie [Audrey Tautou]

Amélie quietly engineers elaborate interventions in other people's lives. Her playful manipulations are usually benevolent, though her avoidance of direct intimacy complicates the picture.

✳
005

Anansi, the Spider

Anansi is a spider figure whose clever bargains and plots recur across Akan and Caribbean storytelling. His victories, mistakes and lessons are not uniform across traditions.

✳
006

Andy Kaufman

Andy Kaufman deliberately blurred performance and reality, testing whether audiences could tell when a joke had started or stopped. His provocations make trickster an interpretive analogy.

✳
007

Ariel from The Little Mermaid

Ariel defies an underwater prohibition and seeks a different life. Her curiosity and rule-breaking resemble aspects of a trickster, but the film primarily casts her as a romantic heroine.

Ārohirohi, Goddess of Mirages

The source names Ārohirohi as a goddess of mirages, but a specific tradition and primary reference remain to be confirmed. A trickster interpretation is provisional.

009

Até, Goddess of Mischief

Atë personifies ruinous delusion and reckless error in Greek tradition. She brings disorder, but describing her simply as a playful goddess of mischief would be misleading.

✳
010

Bacchus/Dionysus, God of Wine, Insanity, Partying

Dionysus crosses boundaries through wine, theater, ecstasy and altered identities. His disruptive power makes him trickster-adjacent, not solely a comic prankster.

✳
011

Bart Simpson

Bart's pranks and defiance challenge family, school and civic authority. His mischief is central to the show's satirical portrayal of everyday institutions.

✳
012

Baubo, Goddess of Mirth

Baubo is associated with ribald humor that breaks Demeter's grief in later accounts. The sacred and humorous contexts require care rather than reduction to a generic trickster.

013

Benjamin Franklin

Benjamin Franklin used fictional personae, maxims and satire as a writer and printer. Calling the statesman a trickster is an editorial reading of those practices.

✳
014

Betelgeuse / Beetlejuice

Beetlejuice uses disguises and outrageous breaches of social rules to manipulate the living. His anarchic comedy is frequently selfish and threatening.

✳
015

Bugs Bunny

Bugs Bunny defeats antagonists through quick wit, costume changes and reversals of power. He is a textbook comic trickster in animation.

✳

Captain Jack Sparrow from Pirates of the Caribbean

Jack Sparrow survives by bluffing, improvising and turning rivals against one another. Disney's character material explicitly describes him as a trickster.

✳
017

Catherine Tate

Catherine Tate's shifting comic personae exaggerate social speech and behavior. The trickster label concerns her performances, not an established trait of her private identity.

✳
018

Cheshire Cat From Alice In Wonderland

The Cheshire Cat confuses Alice through riddles, smiles and abrupt disappearances. His refusal to offer straight answers keeps Wonderland's rules unstable.

Curupira

Dave Grohl

David Bowie

E.T.

E.T. brings wonder and occasional comic mishaps to an ordinary household. He is better read as a playful outsider than a deliberate deceiver.

Edgar Allan Poe

Edgar Allan Poe used irony, hoaxes and unsettling narrators in his literary work. The trickster parallel lies in narrative technique, not in calling the author himself a mythic trickster.

BEHIND THE ARCHIVEEditorial context & sourcing ↗

The Trickster Files brings together mythology, folklore, performance, fiction and popular culture. The trickster label is an editorial interpretation, not a claim that every originating tradition describes these figures in the same way. Images are used to illustrate the subjects; licensing and artwork credits are documented separately.
