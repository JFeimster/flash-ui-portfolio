Anansi

Akan storytelling traditions; also told across West Africa and the Caribbean

A spider storyteller whose clever moves shape tales across Akan and Caribbean traditions. Anansi can win through resourcefulness, but the stories do not always reward every scheme.

1 documented sourceExplore profile ↗
FILE / 02Folklore & oral storytelling
✦

Br'er Rabbit

African American and African diaspora folktale traditions

A small animal protagonist who repeatedly outthinks stronger opponents. The appeal of these tales lies in how a vulnerable character survives through imagination rather than force.

1 documented sourceExplore profile ↗
FILE / 03Folklore & oral storytelling
◆

Coyote (selected traditions)

Several distinct Indigenous storytelling traditions of North America

Coyote features in stories shared by different Native communities, often upsetting expectations through unconventional choices. Those traditions have distinct voices and should not be collapsed into a single version.

2 documented sourcesExplore profile ↗
FILE / 04Divine figures
◈

Èṣù (Eshu)

Yoruba religious traditions

A divine messenger associated with thresholds, crossroads and the possibility of change. Describing Èṣù solely as a prankster would flatten a much broader spiritual role.

1 documented sourceExplore profile ↗
FILE / 05Divine figures
✴

Hermes

Ancient Greek mythology

The Greek messenger of the gods is also associated with travelers and movement between worlds. Stories about his ingenuity and deception give this boundary-crossing deity a trickster dimension.

2 documented sourcesExplore profile ↗
FILE / 06Divine figures
♜

Loki

Norse mythology

Loki is both a source of crises among the Norse gods and an occasional solver of their problems. His shapechanging and verbal ingenuity make the line between help and trouble difficult to hold.

1 documented sourceExplore profile ↗
FILE / 07Culture heroes
✧

Māui

Māori traditions within the wider Polynesian world

Māui's daring feats in Māori stories include bringing islands from the sea and capturing the sun. He is a culture hero whose audacity does more than simply create mischief.

1 documented sourceExplore profile ↗
FILE / 08Folklore & oral storytelling
✳

Nasreddin Hodja

Shared anecdote traditions across Azerbaijan, Central Asia and Türkiye

In anecdotes carried across multiple communities, Nasreddin uses a surprising reply or upside-down solution to expose an assumption. The fool and the teacher can sound remarkably alike.

1 documented sourceExplore profile ↗
FILE / 09Folklore & oral storytelling
✦

Raven (Tlingit storytelling)

Tlingit storytelling traditions, Pacific Northwest

In a Tlingit account of the world's light, Raven transforms himself and tricks his way toward releasing celestial lights. This entry presents one documented tradition, not every Raven narrative.

2 documented sourcesExplore profile ↗
FILE / 10Literature

Reynard the Fox

Medieval European beast epics, especially French and Low Countries traditions

The eloquent fox of medieval beast narratives repeatedly turns a world of stronger animals to his advantage. His exploits became a way to mock institutions and displays of power.

1 documented sourceExplore profile ↗

Sun Wukong (Monkey King)

Chinese literary tradition, notably Journey to the West

The Monkey King of Journey to the West changes form, resists authority and seeks freedom from limits. His fantastic abilities make him both an unruly figure and a formidable traveling companion.
