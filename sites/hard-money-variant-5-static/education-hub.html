Hard Money vs. Traditional Financing

Understanding when to deploy bridge capital versus long-term conventional lending is the key to maximizing cash velocity and project scaling.

PARAMETER PRIVATE HARD MONEY CONVENTIONAL BANKS
Approval Metric Asset value, LTV & property profitability FICO score, debt-to-income (DTI), cash reserves
Funding Velocity 3 to 10 Business Days 45 to 90 Calendar Days
Target Interest Rates 7.99% - 13.00% (Interest-Only) 6.00% - 8.50% (Fully Amortized)
Loan Term Limits 6 Months to 3 Years (Flexible Exit) 15 to 30 Years (Long Term)
Rehab Financing Up to 100% of Rehab Costs (Escrow Draw) Rarely supported, paid out-of-pocket
Property Condition Distressed, raw land, teardowns allowed Must be move-in ready, strict occupancy codes
PRO-TIP: THE VELOCITY OF MULTIPLIER Hard money is not designed for permanent hold strategies. It is used as short-term acquisition and rehabilitation capital. Once the asset value is stabilized, the property is refinanced into a long-term conventional DSCR or bank loan, returning your working capital to seek new acquisitions.

rehab execution

Draw Schedule & Fund Disbursement

Unlike a standard consumer mortgage, your rehab funds are held in a secure escrow account and released in segments called "Draws". Master this flow to prevent construction bottlenecks.

1

Feasibility & SOW (Scope of Work) Approval

Before closing, the lender inspects and approves your itemized list of materials, labor costs, and project milestones. This becomes the legal timeline for fund distribution.

2

Self-Funding Milestone Execution

Lenders issue funds in arrears. You execute Draw Phase 1 (e.g., demolition and rough plumbing) using your cash reserves. Lenders do not wire cash up front for unfinished work.

3

Inspector Audits & Disbursement

Once Phase 1 is done, request a draw. The lender dispatches an inspector to verify completion. Upon approval, escrow releases Draw 1 funds directly to you, replenishing your reserves to start Phase 2.

Strategic Reserve Recommendation Always maintain a working capital buffer representing at least 15% to 20% of your total construction budget to fund materials upfront before your first inspection draw clears.
