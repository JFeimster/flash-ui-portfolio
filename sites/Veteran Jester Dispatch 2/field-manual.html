SOP #01

Establishing Commander's Intent

Leadership isn't about micromanagement; it's about providing the "End State." If your team knows exactly what success looks like, they can navigate the obstacles you haven't even thought of yet.

The Protocol

  • Define the Why: Why are we conducting this operation? If you can't explain it in one sentence, you don't understand it.
  • The End State: Describe the environment after the mission is successful.
  • Left/Right Limits: Define the boundaries. What is strictly forbidden?
CAUTION: Any commander's intent that exceeds 100 words is no longer intent; it's a script. Scripts fail in contact. Intent survives.
SOP #02

The "No-Fail" Briefing

Briefings in the boardroom are often masturbatory exercises in slide-deck design. We don't do that here.

Standard Operating Procedure

  1. Identify the primary threat to the mission.
  2. Assign ownership for that threat to a specific individual.
  3. Conduct a "Back-Brief" where the team tells YOU what they heard.

Tactical Note: The 70% Rule

A good plan violently executed now is better than a perfect plan executed next week. Wait for 100% certainty and you'll be managing a funeral, not a business.

SOP #03

Decentralized Command

Pushing authority down to the lowest possible level. If the CEO is deciding on the color of the marketing flyers, the organization is fundamentally broken.

Implementation Steps

  • Resource: Give them the tools.
  • Release: Give them the authority.
  • Review: Only check-in at predefined milestones or when limits are breached.
SOP #04

Crisis Communication Protocol

When the engine catches fire, that is not the time to form a committee to discuss the merits of fire extinguishers.

URGENT: In a crisis, the person closest to the problem is the leader until someone more senior arrives and explicitly says "I have the lead."

Standard comms format during crisis: SITUATION - ACTION TAKEN - ASSISTANCE REQUIRED. No adjectives. No excuses. Just data.
